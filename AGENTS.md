# 0.5.0 rules: three minimums and the Autopilot bar (binding; supersedes older bullets below)

- What this supersedes. The owner's requests of 6 October 2026 (fewer measures, none redundant; "auto manage the score buffer"; and his field report: "i'm sitting around in downtown Cincinnati and not getting any good offers. my working suspicion is that my approval rating being so low and causing me to basically have no silver/gold/platinum status is potentially to blame"), with his binding Autopilot decisions that day, were built as the 0.5.0 spec. They supersede every older bullet below on: score by area (pure area); the hotspot spoke (both bullets); per item and per stop as rules, and the unknown-item bound; the shared fixed/learned resolver (AreaScore.Floors); adaptive learning, adopt/Undo, Reset and the sparkle (outcome detection stays, and now only counts); the minimums-scale drag and its percentage; auto-accept "respecting strict or area rule and percentage buffer"; fitness trees and the strict "score reference". Those bullets are kept as history and marked "Superseded in 0.5.0" where they stand; where any older text differs from this section, this section wins. Never bring back a retired rule, a learned minimum or a manual scale without a new owner decision.
- Three minimums and max stops (FilterSettings; prefs offer_filter: flat, mile, minute, max_stops). An offer must meet each minimum the user set: minimum pay (cents); pay per mile (cents a mile, multiplied as an exact BigDecimal); and pay per hour of Dasher's own time estimate (the stored per-minute value, unchanged in storage, shown and edited ×60: a knob step of 1¢ a minute is $0.60 an hour, "$15/hr", reason "dollars per hour"). Max stops is optional (off by default), inclusive and never scaled, and unread stops while it is set are REVIEW. Every standalone offer is judged by one strict rule: R100 is the highest of the set money minimums' asks (minimum pay, per mile × miles, per minute × minutes), and the offer is KEEP exactly when pay ≥ ⌈bar × R100 ÷ 100⌉, one ceiling after exact scaling (AreaScore.required100 and roundedCents). Raising a minimum or the bar, or lowering a set max stops, never turns a DECLINE into a KEEP (property-tested). Pay, or a quantity a set minimum needs, unread is REVIEW, never a guess; a separate known failure still declines. Stops and item counts are still read and shown (max stops, the "+$" ceiling, misread checks, tickets, history, reports); no rule uses an item count. Add-ons stay strict at the current bar: the combined route against every minimum and max stops, and the explicitly added pay at least ⌈bar × max(per mile × added miles, per minute × added minutes) ÷ 100⌉ (minimum pay is no increment floor; an unknown increment, or unknown added pay while a rate minimum is set, is REVIEW). Add-ons never count toward Autopilot or acceptance-rate accounting and are never auto-accepted.
- Score = ⌊100 × pay ÷ R100⌋, "% of your minimums": one meaning everywhere (ticket, skyline trees, constellation, screen readers, reports). It is unknown when pay or a needed quantity is unread or no money minimum is set. At bar σ a complete offer within max stops is KEEP exactly when its score ≥ σ, so no label can claim a cutoff the offer missed. Lines written from 0.5.0 store 'score', 'bar', 'auto' and 'model': 2; older lines keep their stored area score, labelled "Area score (retired rules) N%", with no tree. No predicted earnings anywhere: pay per hour is shown only as history ("This is history, not a promise"), the accepts still needed are "about" and called an estimate, and no tier rule is claimed beyond "check the Dasher app".
- The bar (FilterSettings.minimumScalePercent, prefs autopilot_bar_percent) is exactly 100 while Autopilot is off, and a whole number from 50 to 150 while it is on. Only Autopilot moves it, through FilterStore.commitAutopilotBar (compare-and-set under the class lock, only while on), from the scanner thread at a safe point (the safe-point bullet below). FilterStore.save never writes it, and no replay, restore or manual control moves it (the hidden drag, its scroll and set-progress actions and minimum_scale_percent are retired). The user's own change never waits: turning Autopilot off writes 100 at once and hands a decline under way back. The bar scales standalone requirements and both add-on checks; a "+$" ceiling declines at min(bar, 100); max stops is never scaled. Each commit moves at most 20 points down, or at most 5 up (and only 5 minutes and 3 counted offers after the last raise); commits are at least 60 s apart; a 3-point deadband holds the bar, snapping back to exactly 100. A jump (Autopilot turned on, the goal or the minimums changed, Clear history) goes straight to its target, still only at a safe point. Every commit is one "[autopilot] commit A% -> B% (reason)" line with a fixed reason. Automatic changes are never announced and use no live region.
- Autopilot is off by default, and stays off after the update for existing installs. There is one Autopilot control per layout, and none needs a 50/50 split: the constellation's round button where the row of three stood (SCORE_ID: "Auto" over its bar or "Off"; a Switch for screen readers) wherever the constellation is in the sky; the one-line chip in a short window's header and on the driving strip; and, on a whole screen while it is on, the status line in Next match's place (the chip takes it over only where the page has no room for it). A tap while off asks the goal (a toast instead while no pay, per-mile or hourly minimum is set); a tap while on turns it off ("Autopilot off · back to exactly your minimums"); a long press (the chip's too) changes the goal; the status line, the chip's tap and a screen-reader action open the details. The goal chooser is the owner's ("Ask at setup, suggest 70%"): "What matters more?", with Keep a top tier — acceptance rate 70% or more (preselected), Keep a tier — acceptance rate 50% or more, and Pay first — no acceptance-rate goal. One tap applies; the goal is stored as 70, 50 or 0; Autopilot is never on without that choice. Its About text says only that DoorDash computes acceptance rate over your recent offers (it reports the last 100) and that its rewards tiers use acceptance-rate minimums, so check the Dasher app for current requirements; never claim a tier rule beyond that. New installs: the start line offers typical minimums ($4.00 · $1.00/mi · $15/hr, "typical of Cincinnati offers seen in October 2026"; auto-decline stays paused until the mascot turns it on), then the chooser with 70% preselected. Anyone never asked (an existing install too) sees the chooser once, the first time the mascot turns auto-decline on with a money minimum (FilterStore.goalAsked).
- How Autopilot chooses (Autopilot.plan: pure, deterministic, exact whole-number arithmetic; AutopilotRuntime runs it on its own thread when screen reading connects, every 60 s during a dash and after a reading or a change). Acceptance rate comes first, a first-class constraint (the owner's decisions 1–3): the bar is never above the highest bar at which enough of the last 100 counted offers would pass. Enough means the goal + 5 points (the owner's "small margin"); the goal + 10 while recovering (from a rate below the goal until it reaches the goal + 2), with progress shown as an estimate ("AR 9% → 70%: about 61 more accepts"); plus the stall correction, 2 points at a time up to 10, checked every 25 counted offers: added only while the rate carried forward from a Dasher reading stays below the goal and no higher than at the last check (as when passing offers are skipped), taken off again 2 at a time once the rate is at the goal + 5; never more than 95%. Pay first still keeps 1 offer in 5 passing. Pay comes second: within that limit Autopilot picks a bar whose estimated long-run pay per hour is within 2% of the best, preferring the bar it already has. The estimate is renewal-reward (λ·E[pay·1A] ÷ (1 + λ·E[minutes·1A]), Dasher's minutes as trip time), with λ from waiting watched with Dasher on screen, behind a cautious prior of 6 offers an hour with a 2-hour half-life. The bar goes below 100 only for the acceptance-rate need, the pay-first floor or that 2% value rule, and above 100 only through the value rule. Until 20 offers are counted it stays at 100 ("learning"); nothing is planned or committed before the current notice is accepted. It learns nothing from what the user accepts or declines.
- Dasher's acceptance rate is read only from Dasher's decline question, for the app's own declines and the user's, whether Autopilot is on or off: AcceptanceRate.parse on a copy of the labels the confirmation read already holds, parsed off the scanner thread, so the confirmation tap never waits. A reading needs exactly one "N%" label beside acceptance-rate wording; anything else is no reading. Never read it from an account, ratings or earnings screen. Kept on the phone (prefs autopilot): the latest whole percent, when it was seen and the question's offer as a numeric fingerprint; dropped after 7 days and by Clear history; never any label text. The question's own offer counts as one offer since the reading, on the assumption (unverified on a phone) that Dasher shows the rate from before that decline counts. With no fresh reading Autopilot counts from the app's own history: an estimate, shown "~". The rate leaves the phone only in Share report, attached masked diagnostics and Report this offer, never in the summary after a dash: the bar there can show it moved because the rate was below the goal, never the rate itself.
- Exempt declines (the owner's decision 4): when Dasher's decline question says that declining does not lower your acceptance rate, that offer's line gets an AR_EXEMPT step ("Dasher said declining it does not lower your acceptance rate") and the offer is left out of acceptance-rate accounting. The valve: with at least 10 declines in the window and more than half of them marked, the phrase is taken as Dasher's general wording and the marks are ignored ("ar exemptions ignored: …", logged at most once a day). Whether the phrase is a per-offer mark is for the owner to confirm on a phone.
- Auto-accept never goes below the minimums (the owner's decision 5, "even in recovery"). It needs a pay, per-mile or hourly minimum (max stops alone is not enough) and KEEP under AutoAccept.acceptRules: 100% of the user's minimums, or Autopilot's bar when that is higher, both when a candidate is armed and in the recheck before dispatch. An offer that passes only because the bar is below 100% (Decision.belowMinimums) is left to the user and announced as REVIEW (OfferNotificationService.announced): Dasher stays up; a notification or Peek card goes on Offers to check ("…; below your minimums (85%), passed by Autopilot's 82% bar. Yours to accept."), never with the pass chime; the slim tab is amber; the ticket says "Below your minimums: passed by Autopilot's 82% bar. Never auto-accepted."; and it is never armed.
- Nothing is learned, and the retired rules stay retired. No rule changes for any outcome; acceptances and declines are still detected (the counting bullet below) for history, tallies and acceptance-rate accounting only. Retired, with their stored values: the learned minimums (the highest accepted pay + 1¢, best accepted rates, decline-taught floors, ManualDeclines and its prefs file), adopt/Undo, the sparkle and Reset, score by area, the hotspot spoke, per stop (folded into minimum pay as 2 × per stop), per item and its unknown-item bound, and the minimums-scale drag. Axis indexes stay stable: per stop's pin is drawn only as the max stops badge's anchor; HOTSPOT 4 and ITEM 5 are reserved, never drawn, read, scored or exposed (HOTSPOT_SPOKE.md); OfferSnapshot.finalStopHotspotMiles is never set and still parses.
- Migration (FilterStore.migrateToModel2 in load(): idempotent, one apply, run again after a downgrade wrote a retired key). Per stop is folded into minimum pay (max(flat, 2 × per stop), capped); the old percentage is built into each minimum, rounding up so nothing gets looser; the 25 retired keys and offer_filter_manual_declines are deleted; auto-decline is paused when no rule is left; auto-accept is turned off once when the rules changed meaning or only max stops is left; Autopilot starts off with goal 70 and bar 100; one "rules" log line says what changed. The one-time notice "Your rules are simpler now" (after the current notice, until a button is tapped) lists each change that applies. When the migrated minimums would have passed fewer than 1 in 5 of the last 20 or more offers, it offers "Use typical minimums" beside "Keep mine". The user's numbers are never replaced automatically; plain strict rules at 100% with nothing learned get no notice.
- Privacy: Consent.VERSION 14 (never published) carries 0.5.0's new local data: the acceptance-rate reading, Autopilot's settings (prefs offer_filter: on, goal, bar) and state (prefs autopilot: recovery, the stall correction, the last change), the history's 'bar', 'auto' and 'model' fields and the AR_EXEMPT step. Raise it to 15 if 14 ships first. Clear history removes the reading and the last-change note (AutopilotRuntime.cleared, after DecisionLog and the wait records are cleared and before DiagnosticLog); the rules and Autopilot's settings stay.

# Accountless feedback (released as 0.5.0) / end-user GitHub retired

- End-user GitHub is gone from the app: no sign-in, token, GitHub update channel or GitHub report path. The app updates only from the public Render feed (UpdatePolicy accepts only Render addresses; tools/UpdateChannelProbe.java and tools/publish-repo-feed.py check against them); keep package/version/size/SHA-256/original-signer verification unchanged. Repository release files and maintainer tooling may still use GitHub behind the scenes. Never bring back a GitHub connection, pasted token, report outbox or the offer-report fixer.
- LegacyReportingCleanup is permanent and idempotent: it deletes the prefs files github, github_history, reports and dash_diagnostics and files/report-outbox (*.json.tmp included), cancels jobs 7244 and 7245, and only after all of that succeeded writes its durable done flag (a failed step leaves it unset and the next trigger retries; an outbox it cannot list fails closed). It runs off the main thread from UpdateReceiver.updated(), MainActivity.onCreate and both services' connect, logs one generic line ("retired GitHub reporting removed: …"), never a token, login or code, and creates no legacy prefs file on a fresh install.
- Everything users send goes to the accountless feedback service (Feedback, FeedbackOutbox, FeedbackJobService, FeedbackDialogs, FeedbackHttp): the Supabase Edge Function in backend/anonymous-feedback, deployed by hand only (its README; never from CI). No account, name, email, install or device identifier; a fresh random token per submission only ties its parts together. Public clients have no database access and no privileged key; the service stores no raw IP, user agent or account identifier, and only a daily-keyed one-way network value for rate limiting. Do not call this a guarantee of anonymity. Server-side retention is 90 days. The client must keep working with both the deployed function and the hardened one in the repository.
- Consent.VERSION 14 covers this data-flow change (13 was the last published notice; 14 was never published before this). TERMS/PRIVACY/LegalTexts stay byte-equivalent through tools/legal_texts.py, and PRIVACY.md states exactly what each path sends and how long each copy is kept. Until the hardened function is deployed, PRIVACY describes only what both functions do (the deployed one keys its network value with the service's own long-lived key and deletes counts older than two days only as later submissions arrive; its catch-all answers 400 even when storage was unreachable, which the app drops).
- Release gates for 0.5.0 (PUBLIC_BETA_PHONE_GATE.md, "Other launch gates"): deploy the hardening migration and function first; the Render page's legal links go to offerfilter.org/terms/, /privacy/ and /license/ only once the site serves them (tools/mirror_repo_feed.py checks each as it builds the page and links the public source copy meanwhile); the in-app Help row opens https://offerfilter.org/install/ with no fallback, so tools/publish-repo-feed.py refuses to publish until the site serves it (tools/help_page.py); and the user's explicit confirmation of the two flows this release adds beyond what users send themselves: the opt-in summary after each dash (DashSummary, with its hooks in both services) and the stop line's dialog that opens with Attach masked diagnostics on (the user still taps Send). - .70 factual-disclosure correction supersedes older blanket retention/cleanup prose below: logs use a rolling 24-hour window pruned during use; the latest 200 decisions have no age-based expiry; the feedback outbox holds at most 5 submissions for at most 7 days and the last 10 references stay until replaced. - Never starve Dasher (P0 field incident, 6 October 2026: with the screen reader on, Dasher stopped answering its user's taps or drawing: Navigate would not open maps, finished dashes showed $0, the dash could not be ended, Peek's offer details never appeared). Every node read is a call Dasher's own UI thread must serve, so reads are made cheap, not late. The 150 ms quiet gap below stays the only wait before a read that can find a readable offer: a window change, an event whose own words (as Android delivered them; nothing is asked of Dasher) show a sign of an offer, the first event from a window of Dasher's not known yet, and any change after a read showing a sign of an offer are read at once, and a content change after a read showing none waits that gap at most. Any longer wait for a screen that can be read (a cost backoff on costly screens, a watchdog pause) needs the owner's approval first; one read a second during deliveries is the owner's approved exception, (9). Where these differ from the cadence prose below, they supersede it: (1) A map's subtree is never walked: a node whose class name ends in MapView, a SurfaceView or TextureView whose view ID names a map, or one described as "Google Map" or "Map" is counted and its children left unread, unless its own words show an offer's facts ("[screen] map subtree skipped (N children) on Dasher's <kind> screen", once per kind); not while a decline-error recovery or an automatic acceptance is verified (Back and Accept go only by complete reads). On phones a MapView's class name is usually its superclass's and view IDs are not reported, so pruning rests on the "Google Map" description until a real node dump shows Dasher's map. (2) For scheduling only (decisions and outcome counting keep every figure as before), figures a screen Dasher's own words name explains itself are no sign of an offer: its one amount (the in-dash "This dash $0.00", a pickup's "$9.30 this offer", "Total earned") and its time and distance, on the wait for offers, the dash's own screen and summary, the End-dash question, the dash's end or home, a delivery, pickup or route, or navigation, with no offer control or label, new-offer headline, question about declining, countdown, stops, items or "+$" bound on it. Such a screen gets the quiet gap (during a delivery, one read a second: (9)). One amount on a screen none of Dasher's words name is still a sign. (3) An unchanged offer already decided and left to the user (it passes or needs review; the same offer as the read before; no decline, question or automatic acceptance under way) gets the quiet gap, as an unchanged decided offer under takeover does; a decline under way still reads every change at once. (4) A screen too big to read is a possible offer only when the part read shows a sign of one (less figures it explains itself). Either way no automatic action is taken, the tab over it is the slim peek that takes no touches and the guide stays off, and a peek neither goes back over it for want of an offer nor counts it as a peek that found none. With no sign, its changes and clicks are read on the read budget (ReadBudget), as are timers' settling rechecks and the read that brings the tab back after the user's tap on Dasher; acceptance observation waits out a pause. The budget, one for all of Dasher's windows (they share one UI thread): at most 4 such reads a second, 2 at once; 1 a second while a route is stored or the last read was a delivery, pickup, route or navigation screen; the next no sooner than 3 times the last such read's own time after it ended (a quarter of Dasher's time at most), doubling up to 2 s while such reads keep taking 250 ms or more; and when the median over the last 10 reads of each read's slowest single call into Dasher is over 200 ms after a read showing nothing of an offer, none for 3 s, then 6 s, then 10 s while the first read after each pause is still slow ("[screen] yielding to Dasher: median fetch N ms", at most once a minute). Nothing is held back during a decline under way or its question awaited, a decline-error recovery, an automatic acceptance watched or a peek, or for 3 s after an offer's notification asked for a check (which also judges Dasher's slowness afresh); an offer's sign refills the bucket but does not end a pause. (5) From Android 13 a read taken at once asks for Dasher's root with uninterruptible hybrid prefetch, a read after one that showed nothing of an offer (or of an unchanged decided offer) with interruptible prefetch, and a root asked for only to learn whose window it is (another app's, an unknown window, a peek's return check, a paused look) comes alone; an unknown window found to be Dasher's for a read taken at once is asked for again with its nodes. Dasher's window IDs are learned from its own events, and the window watch and every other look outside a read go by Android's list of windows and the windows known by their IDs: an app's root is asked for only to learn whose an unknown window is, once, and Dasher's never (before Android 13 that call also fetched the nodes below the root). (6) Dasher's question is looked for in its other windows only while a decline is under way (its authority, or the wait over a question it confirmed), not for the rest of the decline's two-minute episode. (7) A rules change, or a pause or resume from the tab, asks for one read at a time, 150 ms after the last at the soonest (a screen reader stepping a knob or the max stops badge saves at every step; the minimums-scale drag that also did is retired in 0.5.0), and resets neither the budget nor the watchdog. (8) Once a minute while Dasher is read: "[screen] read load: R reads/min (fact-free F), nodes N, slowest S ms, median fetch M ms, truncated T, pruned map subtrees P, yields Y" (counts and times only, trimmed first with the timing lines). Logged durations of 10 s or more group their digits ("12,345 ms") so the log's masking cannot take them for a ZIP code; the first-step decline line's wait runs from the first change its read took in. accessibility_service_config.xml is unchanged on purpose: Android never merges content changes (a notificationTimeout only delays them, and would delay and merge an offer's window change), each event type and flag serves offer reads, the hand-back or the window watch, and flagReportViewIds would make Dasher's UI thread look up every node's resource name (so the view-ID map rule stays dormant on phones). Window-state events are not coalesced (a window change is read at once). (9) The owner's decision (6 October 2026, asked in chat, "Once a second" chosen over the 150 ms rule for this case): while a delivery is under way (a route is stored, or the last read was a delivery, pickup, route or navigation screen), a content change or a click after a read that showed nothing of an offer waits for one read a second (ReadBudget.calmDueAt), and nothing more: neither the cost backoff nor the watchdog holds such a screen back further. So an add-on Dasher draws there by a change alone is first declined up to about a second late (measured: 980 ms), while a window change, an event whose own words show a sign of an offer, an offer's notification, a decline under way and a peek are still read at once. NeverStarveStressTest holds these numbers (delivery and pickup screens: 61 reads a minute, about 2% of Dasher's time).

- Paused is a safe mode (same incident): with auto-decline off or no rule set (no rule left pauses auto-decline), nothing of Dasher's is read, not one node and not a click's node either; only Android's list of windows, with Dasher's windows known by the IDs its own events and earlier reads named (a window none named yet is asked once whose it is, by its root alone). What the last read made of the screen stands until the next event (Dasher's, or any change in Android's list of windows), so a tab tapped to pause stays as it was and takes the tap that resumes; from then on the tab follows Dasher placed as over a possible offer, since nothing read can say there is none: the slim peek that takes no touches, and no guide (a pause is then undone from Offer Filter's homepage). With no rule set yet nothing was read, so the tab is that peek from the start. The notification path still knows whether Dasher is on screen. The status reads "Paused: Offer Filter is not reading Dasher" (the name from AppName.NAME); "[screen] not reading Dasher while paused (auto-decline is off|no rule is set)" and "[screen] reading Dasher again" are logged once each way. A pause ends what was under way (decline authority, automatic acceptance, the question's polls, settling, outcome observation, a peek (Dasher left as it is; one Dasher never came up for announces its offer as such a launch does), the Back to map chip and the slim bar's verdict tint); while paused a card's watch sends no own notification tap of Dasher's (no read can say the offer did not show) and the chip's tap opens nothing (no fresh read can show the wait for offers). The decision history then records nothing read from Dasher's screen while paused (no PAUSED line from a screen read; Dasher's notifications are judged and recorded as before, with no rule set as with auto-decline off: left to the user, REVIEW "no rule is set", never a pass, its chime or a payless card beside Dasher's own), the outcome steps and tallies see nothing of the screen (and since 0.5.0 nothing is learned from any outcome), a dash's end on screen is not seen until reads resume, and the screen-on lease stays off.
