# Offer Filter

A local Android assistant for filtering DoorDash delivery offers, with optional auto-accept. Not made by or affiliated with DoorDash. Read [Risks](#risks) before using it. Offer Filter is beta software for Cincinnati Dashers. The source is public (MIT License) at https://github.com/Dillxn/dasher-offer-filter. From 0.4.29 to 0.4.37 it showed as "Dash Buddy"; that name is retired. The package (com.local.dasherfilter) and signer never changed, so every version updates over the last. Android 15 or newer (from 0.6.1; 0.6.0 was the last for Android 8–14); release build targets Android 15.

The home page identifies the latest or selected offer and its recorded outcome. Tap that caption for its ticket (what the rules decided, what happened and why); tap a count to inspect its newest matching offer. Only the mascot pauses or resumes filtering. Your rules are three minimums (pay, per mile, per hour) and an optional max stops, set on the constellation, and [Autopilot](#autopilot)'s chip at the start of the status line turns it on or off (it is on by default). Over the skyline, the offer clock shows your offers by time of day, under your current rules, with an Autopilot score when its working minimums have followed an acceptance (tap it for each three hours); it is history, not a promise. While Autopilot is on, its status line stands where Next match would; otherwise a numerical wait estimate appears only during positively observed waiting with enough qualifying evidence.

**Navigate** remains available in split screen, at every window size. It opens the best recorded offer-arrival area, nearby gas, or gas-price comparison in Google Maps, Maps Go or Waze, with a browser fallback. Historical area rates include sample counts and are not live hotspots or earnings predictions; station selection and current prices belong to the map provider. Arrival is not currently detected, so this does not automatically return from a map after arriving.

Day, Night, System and Auto change only the app's own screens; the home-screen icon stays as installed, because swapping it removed pinned icons on common launchers. If no launcher entry is enabled at all, the day entry is enabled again (after boot, an update, or at most once a minute while the service runs). If the icon disappeared under 0.4.68–0.4.72, add it back from the app drawer.

- **Not while driving.** Don't handle your phone while driving: set your rules before you drive and pull over to look at offers. It is not a safety device.
- **Your data.** What it reads, keeps and sends, and when, is in [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md).

[TERMS.md](TERMS.md) and [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) carry their own effective dates and versions; the terms use Ohio law. No lawyer review is claimed. ## What it actually does

When a real offer screen is visible in Dasher, Accessibility reads the displayed payout, mileage, duration and stop count. A known enabled-rule failure can request Decline immediately and handle the subsequent confirmation (with **Decline after 10 seconds** on, after the wait below). This is a UI request, not proof of server acceptance.

Reading never holds up Offer Filter's own screen. Each read asks Android for the windows and walks up to 1,500 of Dasher's screen elements, every one a call into Dasher (0.2 to 0.7 seconds a read on a slow phone), and Dasher sends many events a second while its map or countdown moves. All of that (every look at the windows, every read, every tap, the confirmation, the touch takeover and the sound) runs on the screen reader's own thread; the main thread, which Offer Filter's screen uses, only notes each event and hands it over, and a read already queued is not queued again. A window change is read at once, and so is every change while the last read showed any sign of an offer (pay, a distance, Accept or Decline, a confirmation), a decline is under way, or a screen is still settling. While nothing is up, the first content change after 150 ms of quiet is read at once too; the changes that follow within 150 ms of that read (the map redrawing, a clock ticking) are read together, once, 150 ms after it. So an offer that opens as a new screen, or that the last read already saw part of, is declined at once, while an offer Dasher draws by a content change alone, within 150 ms of a read that found nothing, is declined up to 150 ms later than it could be. During a delivery (a route stored, or a delivery, pickup, route or navigation screen) a change that shows nothing of an offer is read once a second at most (your choice), so Dasher's map and route screens stay responsive: an add-on Dasher draws there by a content change alone may be declined up to about a second later, while one that opens as a new screen, whose own words show it, or whose notification arrives is read at once. Each first Decline line in the diagnostic log says what started its read and how long that read waited. The history, the last status and Offer Filter's cards are handled on the main thread in order with Dasher's notifications, so a screen reading and a notification of the same offer are still one line with no card left behind.

**Dasher comes first** (the P0 fix of 6 October 2026: with Offer Filter on, Dasher had stopped answering its own taps, so Navigate would not open maps, finished dashes showed $0, the dash could not be ended and Peek's offer details never drew). Every element read is a call Dasher's own screen thread must answer, so reads are made cheap, never late for an offer. The inside of a map Offer Filter recognizes on Dasher's screen is not walked (a map described as "Google Map" or "Map", or a map view, is counted and skipped, unless its own words show an offer's figures, and never while a failed decline's recovery or an automatic Accept is checked, which go only by complete reads); whether Dasher's real maps are recognized depends on how Dasher draws them, still to be checked on a phone. The figures a screen explains itself ("This dash $0.00", a pickup's "$9.30 this offer", a route's time and distance) are no sign of an offer. A screen too big to read in full that shows no sign of an offer is read on a budget (at most four reads a second, and fewer while each read is slow), and when Dasher is slow to answer, such reads pause for a few seconds ("[screen] yielding to Dasher"). None of this delays an offer: a window change, an event whose own words show an offer, Dasher's offer notification, a decline under way and a peek are read at once. Which app is in front is told from Android's list of windows alone, never by asking Dasher. Once a minute, the diagnostic log has a read-load line (reads, elements, slowest call, map subtrees skipped: counts and times only, never text). While auto-decline is paused, or no rule is set, Dasher is not read at all (see below).

Offer reads come first. Every element read is a call Dasher's own screen thread has to answer, and that thread is busiest exactly while an offer slides in, so nothing that can wait gets in an offer's way. A window change, and any of Dasher's events while an offer or its confirmation is up, a decline is under way, or one was seen in the last 3 seconds, is read ahead of anything else waiting. The work that can wait (the half-second look at Android's list of windows, reading around a tap, noting what came after an offer) does not run while an offer or confirmation is up or a decline is under way, runs only after the read Dasher's events asked for, and stops at its next call into Dasher when Dasher sends another event. While an offer is up, a tap on Dasher's screen is named only by what Android says about it and by the offer's own Accept and Decline buttons that the read found, without reading around it. After the first Decline tap, Dasher's "Are you sure?" is looked for every 100 ms for 3 seconds: Android's list of windows is checked (that asks Android, not Dasher), and a bounded part of the screen is read when it or Dasher's events changed, or once a second when no event arrives; no read looks at the same window twice, and while the question is awaited, a read of the offer stops when a new window opens, so the question's own window is read at once. It is tapped the moment it is found, and the diagnostic log says how long after the first tap it was found and tapped. Every read slower than 150 ms while an offer or confirmation is (or was just) up or a decline is under way is noted in the diagnostic log, with where the time went (listing the windows, fetching the screen's root, walking its elements, and how many); slow reads while nothing is up are noted once a minute at most. So a shared report shows the phone's real timings.

While Dasher still shows the same offer after a successful Decline request, first-step retries are spaced about 2–3 seconds apart, with at most four requests and no extension of the original countdown deadline. A scheduled recheck also runs when Dasher sends no new screen event. A user touch, lock, changed offer or fresh countdown ends that authority. A hung blank screen alone never permits Back. After a fresh Dasher error toast tied to the app's first Decline, a recognized empty/map-only screen with Dasher alone in the foreground can receive at most two Back recoveries within the original deadline, one per failed attempt. The same offer must be freshly read and verified before retrying. This recovery stops for your touch, another offer, lock or an app switch. Actual Samsung/Dasher toast and dimmed-map behavior still needs a real-phone check.

During an active dash with auto-decline on and a rule set, the app keeps an already unlocked screen from timing out, whatever app is in front (Dasher, a map, Offer Filter or another), so Peek can still bring Dasher up for a background offer. It holds only on fresh signs of the dash: something of it seen in the last 15 minutes (an offer or its notification, Dasher's wait for offers, a pickup or delivery screen), or Dasher in front showing one of those now. A route left stored, a dash whose end was never seen, or Dasher's home before a dash does not hold it. The short renewable lease ends when the dash is paused or ends, auto-decline is paused, a call starts, battery saver is on, or the battery is at 20 % or less and not charging. It does not wake the phone, unlock it, or change system timeout or brightness settings. Pressing the power button still turns the screen off, and Peek pauses while your phone is locked. Ending your dash in Dasher (End dash, then End dash on its question) lets the screen time out again at once.

The confirmation step belongs only to the offer that was declined. A confirmation needs a prompt ("Are you sure…", "Decline offer?") or a way back ("Cancel", "Go back"); a lone Decline button is how a new offer looks while it is still being drawn. A screen whose pay, distance, time or stops contradict the declined offer is a different offer and is judged on its own. Once the tapped confirmation closes, the authority ends.

In the background, a notification containing sufficient explicit offer data can be evaluated. Passing offers receive a selective Offer Filter alert when a new app bell is needed. An offer that cannot be judged uses **Offers to check**. An offer that cannot be judged gets no card of Offer Filter's while Dasher's own notification of it is there to tap and alerts by itself (Android ranks it to pop up, or to sound, and Do Not Disturb lets it through), whether or not Android says it sounded: such a card would only repeat Dasher's. The **Offers to check** card (ringing once) appears while Dasher's notification is gone, cannot be tapped or cannot alert by itself (its channel silenced or turned down, or held back by Do Not Disturb), or when Android cannot say; a quiet Peek that cannot finish follows the same rule. A passing offer's card gives way to Dasher's tappable notification when no new bell is needed. A card that carries what Peek read and need not ring pops up without a sound of its own on **Offer details** and counts down with the offer. The card names the store Dasher's notification names ("Taco Bell offer: open Dasher to check it"), or says "DoorDash offer" when it names none. Dasher's "New Delivery!" / "New Order: Go to …" is a new offer, never a sign that you accepted one. When Android shows that Dasher's own notification of that offer sounded, Offer Filter does not ring again. Peek can still leave a useful card with the observed offer facts. Once the screen has read that offer (whatever it decided, and whether or not the card rang), its card is cleared, except for the observed-offer card Peek leaves when returning during navigation; ordinary cards also clear when you come back to the same offer later, and it does not come back when Android reconnects the notification listener; a card for an offer the screen never read stays, also when you pause, resume or change the rules. Dasher's notification of an offer the screen read moments before gets no card at all. Dasher's own notification is never touched for either. Tapping a card opens Dasher the way its launcher icon does, so Dasher comes forward with the offer it is showing (nothing in it is reset), in its own half when it is already beside Offer Filter in split screen (or into the other half when the screen is split without Dasher), and the card goes. The tap goes through a screen of Offer Filter's that never shows, because Android 12 and later let a notification tap start an app's screen only directly, not through a service or broadcast. Cards used to open Dasher's own notification intent, which on some phones opened a screen that could not find the offer; it is still used if Dasher has no launcher entry to open. Card taps open Dasher only at your request; the default-on Peek setting below can also open it automatically. A background notification with only a merchant name cannot establish payout, distance, or whether the order passes.

If a known-failing background offer exposes a DoorDash-owned Decline action that opens no screen, the app can request it. Otherwise it can hide the notification only. **Hiding a notification does not decline the order**; it may remain pending until acted on or expired.

## Your rules: three minimums

Strict mode requires each active minimum; Area mode, the default, allows stronger measures to make up for weaker ones in a combined score. A saved minimum at zero is off unless enabled acceptance learning supplies that measure. Max stops stays a hard limit in either mode.

- **Pay per stop** (0.6.12; it was the minimum pay, and keeps its number): what an offer must pay for each stop Dasher shows, the pickup included (0.6.14). Dasher counts a single order as 2 stops, so $5 a stop asks $10 of a single order and $15 of 3 stops. An offer whose stops weren't read goes to review, unless its pay misses even one stop's worth (or another minimum), which declines it.
- **Pay per mile:** pay ÷ the miles Dasher shows.
- **Pay per hour:** pay per hour of Dasher's own time estimate for the trip (its minutes). It is saved as cents per minute, so its knob moves in $0.60/hr steps; $15/hr is 25¢ a minute. It covers shopping time too, since that is in Dasher's minutes.
- **Max stops** (optional, off by default): the most stops an offer may have, inclusive. Stops count pickups plus drop-offs, as DoorDash shows them: a single order is 2 stops and a double is 4. A "(N stops)" breakdown beside pickups or drop-offs is not the total. Max stops is never scaled, and an offer whose stops weren't read goes to review while it is set.

**Required pay in Strict mode** is the highest of what the active minimums ask, `max(pay per stop × stops, miles × per mile, hours × per hour)`, times the bar, rounded up to the next cent. Before any Autopilot acceptance adjustment, this uses the higher of each saved and enabled learned requirement. The bar is exactly 100% with Autopilot off. For example, without learned bests or working adjustments, with the typical minimums ($2.00/stop, $1.00/mi, $15/hr), a single order (2 stops) of 6.6 mi and 27 min must pay $6.75 (27 minutes at $15/hr asks the most; 2 stops × $2.00 ask $4.00); at an 82% bar it must pay $5.54. Miles are multiplied exactly and costs use overflow-safe whole cents. When an offer doesn't show the miles or minutes a set minimum needs, or its pay, it goes to review, never keep or decline, unless another known failure (too many stops, say) already declines it. Conflicting, oversized, malformed, negative, ranged and ambiguous numbers never become exact facts. With the other rules held fixed, raising the working bar never lets through an offer it declined before (this is tested). With Autopilot on, an eligible personal acceptance can independently replace its working requirements, as described below.

**Score.** Strict mode uses the weakest active measure; Area mode combines the active measures by area. The offer passes when its score meets the bar, subject to max stops and readable facts. Before independent adjustments, 100% refers to the resolved saved and enabled learned requirements. After an Autopilot acceptance adjustment, the ticket says **Autopilot score**: its reference is the adjusted working shape, and its bar applies once. For example, an offer whose measures set the working minimums at an 82% bar scores 82% under that shape. A later change to the bar moves those requirements together. Tickets and skyline trees keep each offer's recorded rules and labels; the constellation and offer clock describe the current rules. Offers decided before 0.5.0 keep their retired-rule label and have no tree.

**Starting out.** Setup offers **Start auto-decline**, which saves $2.00 per stop ($4.00 for a single order), $1.00 per mile and $15 per hour and starts filtering. **Set my own** opens the homepage with the knobs beckoning. Changing a knob keeps the on or paused state; the mascot pauses or resumes filtering.

**Saved and learned minimums.** Learn is on by default. A confirmed offer you accept yourself can independently raise the learner's best pay per stop, per mile and per minute; it never lowers them. The learned pay requirement includes its existing one-cent margin. Learn off keeps those records but excludes them from judging offers. Adopt copies the learned values into your saved knobs, with Undo. These bests and your saved knobs stay separate from Autopilot's working adjustments. The 0.5.0 migration retired the former adaptive system; the current separate learner returned in 0.6.0, Area mode became the default in 0.6.27, and independent Autopilot working adjustments begin in 0.6.29. Historical decisions retain the rules and reasons they were given.

## Autopilot

Autopilot is on by default, with **pay first** as its default goal. It adjusts the working minimums used for standalone offers. Its shared **bar**, from 10% to 150% (from 0.6.32; 50% to 150% before), continues to respond to the chosen goal and recent offers; from 0.6.29, each new offer you accept yourself can also reshape its working minimums independently.

- **Each acceptance sets each readable active minimum, up or down.** With Autopilot on, a positively confirmed personal standalone acceptance sets its pay per stop to that offer's pay divided by the stops Dasher showed, pickup included; its pay per mile to pay divided by the shown miles; and its pay per hour to pay divided by Dasher's shown trip time. A higher accepted measure raises that working minimum and a lower one lowers it. The newest acceptance replaces earlier levels, even if an older acceptance was better. For example, with an 82% bar, accepting $6.60 for 2 stops, 6 miles and 12 minutes sets the actual working requirements to $3.30/stop, $1.10/mi and $33/hr. Accepting $9 for 3 stops, 3 miles and 18 minutes next sets them to $3/stop, $3/mi and $30/hr: pay per stop and per hour fall while per mile rises.
- **Total pay counts a quarter of the score (0.6.31).** The latest acceptance also keeps its total pay. In Area mode, while its working minimums apply, an offer's required pay is weighted three quarters by those working rates and one quarter by that acceptance's total pay at the bar (`required = R^¾ × T^¼`). A bigger offer a little under the accepted rates can pass, and a smaller one at them can be declined; an offer exactly like the acceptance meets the bar exactly. For example, after accepting $22.70 for 2 stops, 8.7 mi and 27 min at a 50% bar, $27.00 for 3 stops, 7.0 mi and 62 min needs $26.17 (51%), where it needed $27.43 (49%). The total is the offer's own pay, never pay per stop times stops, and no spoke shows it. Strict mode, add-ons and auto-accept don't use it, and working minimums from before 0.6.31 have none until your next acceptance.
- **Missing facts stay missing.** An unread or zero distance or time cannot supply a rate. An unread stop count cannot supply pay per stop. Such a measure keeps its previous working value; an inactive measure stays inactive. Misread offers, automatic accepts, add-ons, uncertain outcomes, expired offers and declines cannot create these adjustments. Older history is not replayed as new acceptance evidence. Max stops is unchanged and never scaled.
- **Saved knobs and learned bests stay separate.** Autopilot changes neither. Its working values can go below either saved or learned requirements, and can rise above them. This operates with Learn on or off. The existing learner, when on, still records independent new bests only. Changing your own minimums, max stops, score mode, learning setting or goal clears the working adjustments; Adopt, a pause and a same-goal off/on toggle preserve them, including an already confirmed pending update. Turning Autopilot off returns to the saved and enabled learned requirements; turning it back on with the same goal restores the retained working values and prior bar.
- **The bar still moves the working requirements together.** An acceptance leaves the displayed bar unchanged and sets the actual working amounts to the accepted rates at that bar. Those rates are stored exactly, without rounding them to the knobs' steps; a later bar change applies once. Tap Auto for the current dollar amounts; ≈ marks a rate rounded up for display. The latest acceptance holds the shared bar at least at its value at that acceptance for the next 24 counted offers, across dashes. A newer acceptance replaces that hold as well as the observed active rates; an older higher accepted score does not prevent a new lower setting. Once the hold ends, the working references remain until replaced or cleared, and normal bar planning continues.
- **An offer you decline yourself raises the bar just above it (0.6.37).** The owner, 23 October 2026: "if i decline an offer then autopilot should raise my mins to its". With Autopilot on and auto-decline not paused, a standalone offer you decline yourself (your Decline tap or Dasher's decline question, counted once Dasher goes back to the wait for offers or shows another offer) holds the shared bar at least one point above that offer's Autopilot score, up to 150%, for the next 24 counted offers, across dashes, even during an acceptance's hold; offers that score no better are declined meanwhile. It only raises: it never lowers the bar and changes no working minimum. Your next acceptance replaces it with its own hold, a decline from before your latest acceptance holds nothing, and the changes that clear the working adjustments end it. Add-ons, skipped stores' offers, offers over max stops, misread offers and offers with an unread figure don't count, nor do the app's own declines or an offer left to time out. For example, a $9.95 declined at 39% with the bar at 36% holds it at 40%, and an $8.30 scoring 60% still passes. Auto's details say "you declined an offer that scored 39%, so offers no better are declined".
- **Its goal and controls.** Tap the Auto chip for details and **Turn off**; long press it for **Pay first**, **Protect my acceptance rate — 70% or more**, or **Protect my acceptance rate — 50% or more**. The acceptance-rate choices are opt-in. About acceptance rate explains that DoorDash counts recent offers; check Dasher for its current tier requirements. The shared chip and status line remain available at every window size.
- **Acceptance rate and pay planning.** Autopilot reads the rate on Dasher's usual decline question and keeps the latest reading for up to 7 days. With no fresh reading it estimates from your own history, shown with “~”. For an acceptance-rate goal it aims to keep enough of your last 100 counted offers passing to reach the goal and then remain 5 points above it; recovery keeps 10 points more until the estimate is 2 points above the goal. If recovery stalls because passing offers are skipped, it can lower the bar another 2 points every 25 offers, up to 10. Pay first normally keeps 1 offer in 5 passing. A latest-acceptance hold, or a decline's (0.6.37), can prevent the drop that either target would otherwise need. Offers Dasher says do not lower your acceptance rate are excluded. Autopilot only permits offers: your acceptance rate rises when you accept them.
- **Later changes happen between offers.** With at least 20 recorded offers, the existing planner weighs recent pay, Dasher's time estimates and observed waiting to choose a bar near the best estimated pay per hour within its passing-share goal. Ordinary raises are at most 5 points, at least 5 minutes and 3 offers apart; ordinary drops are at most 20 points, at least a minute apart. A confirmed acceptance adjustment waits for the same safe point if an offer or action is still up; the details say it is waiting. No change is committed during an offer, decline, takeover, automatic Accept or Peek, or without the current notice. Off a dash, eligible changes can apply at once. No adjustment is announced aloud.
- **Below the saved or learned minimums remains yours to decide.** An offer permitted only by Autopilot's working adjustments receives the review treatment, has no passing chime, and cannot be auto-accepted. The ticket and card explain that the working minimums permitted it; this can happen even with a displayed bar at or above 100%. Area mode still combines measures, Strict mode still checks each, and the add-on and auto-accept safeguards below stay strict.
- **Where you see the working requirements.** Auto's details list pay per stop, per mile and per hour and the most recent acceptance. The constellation's dashed purple shape shows the actual working ask, including at a 100% bar when the axes differ; saved knobs and learned bests keep their own shapes. A ticket's **How this offer was judged** shows the recorded saved, learned and Autopilot requirements. Adjusted scores are named **Autopilot score**. Existing history keeps its original basis and labels. These are explanations of the rules, not predicted earnings.

From 0.5.1 to 0.6.20 Autopilot also grew saved minimums after a sustained high bar. That growth and its switch ended in 0.6.21. An earlier growth keeps its one-time note with Undo and OK until acknowledged or the saved minimums change. The 0.6.29 working adjustments do not restore that saved-minimum growth.

Autopilot's planning margins were tuned in simulation; the new acceptance behavior still needs a real-phone check. How Dasher draws its decline question and whether the shown rate already includes the pending decline also need phone verification.

## Add-ons and unclear pay

Add-on offers are not fresh standalone orders, so they use the saved rules strictly, without learned standalone bests or independent Autopilot working adjustments. They are judged in two parts, at the current shared bar: the combined route against every minimum and max stops, and the explicitly added pay against `max(added miles × per mile, added minutes × per hour)`. Minimum pay is not asked of the added pay. With a per-mile or per-hour minimum set, an add-on whose added pay, miles or minutes wasn't shown goes to review unless the combined route already fails. Bare numbers do not establish incremental meaning. Old accepted-route travel totals are not subtracted from new totals to invent added miles or time: the route may already be partly completed. A missing active route is not permission to apply the standalone rule to the add-on. Confirmed pickup or completion progress invalidates old travel estimates, and recognized idle screens clear route context; route context can also be cleared by hand. Add-ons are never auto-accepted and never count toward Autopilot.

A busy offer is read at Dasher's total, the owner's choice ("Included", 21 October 2026): a bare "+$X" label directly beside an offer's one total "$Y". Only the offer's own Decline or Accept button, its countdown or the "Very busy" badge may sit between the two, as on Dasher's screen ("Very busy, +$1, Decline, $5.75"); any other label between them keeps them apart. Dasher calls that total "Guaranteed earnings for completing the offer", so it is the offer's pay, the +$ included, however many stops or orders the offer has, and the offer is judged like any other. A "+$" with words ("+$2.00 Peak Pay"), two "+$" amounts, a second total, a rate, labeled pay, a per-order or up-to qualifier, added travel or add-on wording leaves pay unknown ("pay not found"), never guessed. Notifications never use this reading, since their text may be cut short.

## Optional auto-accept

**Auto-accept matching offers** starts off in Settings, including on existing installs. Enabling it requires a separate confirmation because it can commit you to a delivery. It needs a pay, per-mile or hourly minimum (max stops alone is not enough), and it takes only an offer that strictly meets the saved and enabled learned minimums at 100%, or at the shared bar when that is higher, as well as independent Autopilot working minimums wherever they are stricter. An offer permitted only below those saved or learned requirements is left to you. Updating to 0.5.0 turns it off once if your rules changed meaning. Add-ons, incomplete offers and offers with an unclear or nearly expired countdown stay yours. A fresh reread and a working touch watch must establish at least 0.7 seconds without touch before a single node-targeted Accept request. Touch, lock, changing rules or losing the verified Dasher window cancels the attempt. It never retries an uncertain Accept with Back. A request is not server confirmation.

Before persistence or dispatch, a content event during the final check can request at most two new complete reads of the same offer. They preserve the original identity, rules, notification generation, quiet interval and countdown deadline; no stale node is clicked. Changed or incomplete evidence still cancels. Once suppression has been persisted, a changed final check permanently stops that offer as before; no second Accept request is introduced.

Nothing is learned from an automatic acceptance, and it never changes Autopilot's working minimums. Your own positively confirmed acceptances can teach the independent learner while Learn is on and set Autopilot's separate working requirements while Autopilot is on, as described above. An automatic acceptance is marked “Accepted after an automatic Accept request” on its history line only to identify where it came from.

## Peek at background offers

**Peek at background offers** is on by default in Settings, after you accept the current notice. A fresh background notification can briefly bring Dasher forward so the normal filter can read the actual offer. Accept is only requested if you separately enabled auto-accept and the freshly read offer passes all its guards. A store name alone still proves no payout, distance or result.

The phone must be unlocked and quiet for about 0.7 seconds, with no touch or keyboard. Android tells Offer Filter only when a finger lands, never how long it stays down, so a touch counts as under way for 2 seconds after it lands and Dasher never opens in the middle of a pan of the map. If that quiet does not arrive within three seconds there is no peek, and the offer is left to Dasher's notification (and Offer Filter's card, when one is due). Peek skips when its checks find a call, microphone/camera use, screen pinning, split or floating windows, a system picker, an unknown app, a recent acceptance, a takeover or an update being installed. Its touch watch must be available. Old notifications, reconnects and ordinary updates do not start it, and neither does DoorDash re-posting the notification of an offer you already had in Dasher (read on screen, or notified while Dasher was in front), or of one a pickup, delivery or route screen followed (you may have accepted it), for a minute after its first post unless Dasher showed the wait for offers or the dash's end: opening Dasher for it would pull Dasher back over the map you opened from it.

**Locked phone.** Peek needs the phone on and unlocked. An offer whose notification comes while the screen is off or the phone is locked is remembered (in memory only) and looked at when you unlock within 40 seconds of it, if DoorDash's notification of it is still up (an update of the same offer counts) and you haven't opened Dasher yourself in the meantime (a tap on one of our cards, or Dasher's own notification on the lock screen); its quiet 0.7 seconds start after the unlock. If the phone locks during a peek, the peek waits in memory up to 60 seconds: unlock in time and it carries on from a fresh look at Dasher, going back only after a quiet moment, while a touch or a tap on Dasher after the unlock leaves Dasher up. During an active dash the screen is kept from timing out (above), so this matters mostly when you lock the phone yourself.

**Shade down.** An offer whose notification comes while the notification shade or another of Android's own screens is in front (no app's window active, so Peek can't tell what to return you to) is remembered the same way, in memory only, and looked at as soon as the screen reader finds an app in front again, if it's still fresh (10 seconds from its notification), its notification is still up and you haven't opened Dasher yourself in the meantime (from the shade, its icon or one of our cards). From 0.6.39; before that such an offer was left to Dasher's notification, its pay unread.

After a decline, Peek returns only when its own confirmation was requested (or the add-on's single Decline) and Dasher shows a recognized completion screen, or your route again (a pickup's own "$9.30 this offer" is not an offer). If navigation is detected from a stored route, Google Maps, Maps Go or Waze, or car mode, passing and unclear offers return to the previous app or map with a card showing the figures read, under the normal ring rules; an offer that passes only below your minimums goes back as one to check. Otherwise those offers stay in Dasher for you. A touch on Dasher, a card tap or a switch of apps cancels the automatic return; a touch on your map made before Dasher appeared (provably, by its time) doesn't stop a completed automatic decline from taking you back. Another app's notification sliding in at the top doesn't cancel the return; pulling the shade down does. A recognized screen with no offer can return after four seconds, but only once DoorDash's notification of the offer is gone (the offer was withdrawn): while it is still up, Peek waits for the offer to draw. An unreadable screen never returns.

**When Dasher opens without the offer** (the 0.4.72 report: its details appeared only after split screen). After 2.5 seconds with none of the offer's figures (an empty screen, or only its buttons or headline), Peek taps Dasher's own notification of that offer, once. If the offer still doesn't show 5 seconds later, Peek ends: while you navigate it takes you back to your map first, otherwise Dasher stays up, and a card says "Dasher didn't show this offer when it opened. Tap to open it." (its tap opens Dasher's own offer screen first, and Dasher's launcher if nothing comes up). It pops up even with Dasher left open, and it never counts as a peek that found nothing. The same card comes when Peek's 20 seconds run out with no figures read, saying "Couldn't read this offer on Dasher's screen" when the screen was too big to read or showed Dasher's question. A card you tap yourself gets the same fallback: if Dasher comes up in front without the offer's figures, its own notification is tapped once, never while auto-decline is paused.

Peek waits up to six seconds for Dasher to open and ends after twenty seconds from opening, including any offer it follows. It allows at least five seconds between peeks and at most six starts or followed offers per ten minutes. Two launches Dasher never came up for, or three offers withdrawn before Dasher showed them, in a row within half an hour, pause it until your next dash or for 15 minutes; after an error, until your next dash or until you tap Resume. The homepage says "Peek paused: <why>" with Resume. Locks, touches, calls, timeouts and offers that expired while the phone was locked never count, and Peek never turns its Settings switch off. Pausing auto-decline ends a peek under way, leaving Dasher as it is. The previous app's identifier is held only in memory during Peek and its return check (a navigation app's a little longer, for Back to map), never written to logs or sent; no text in that app is read. See [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md).

These are the implemented rules, not a claim of a verified phone release. Background launch, touch detection, audio and return behavior still need checks on real phones, including Samsung/Android 16.

## Sound, vibration and missed-offer tradeoffs

Keep DoorDash notifications allowed. With its offer channel set to Silent, declined offers stay quiet and Offer Filter's own card is the alert for an offer it cannot judge. Offer Filter cannot change another app's notification settings.

Dasher also rings and vibrates for an offer with its own sound, outside its notifications, until the offer closes. **Quiet Dasher while declining** (Settings; on by default) turns down the media and alarm streams while a filtered offer is being declined on screen. During Peek, it touches only the alarm stream, leaving media and navigation voice alone.

- **What it touches.** Only the media and alarm streams, only while that declined offer or its own confirmation is showing, and only when something other than navigation or a call is playing on them.
  - The alarm stream can't be muted, so it goes to its lowest level instead.
  - The ringer, notification and system streams are never touched, because muting them switches the whole phone to vibrate.
- **When it gives way.** Nothing is touched while a call rings or is in progress, and a call that starts mid-decline brings everything back at once. A next offer on screen, a new offer notification, or a passing offer's bell does the same.
- **Restoring.** Everything comes back when the decline ends, after 20 seconds at most, or at the next start if the app died in between.

Outside Peek, navigation voice shares the media stream, so if Dasher's ring plays there, directions can be quiet for those seconds too. Peek leaves that stream alone. If Dasher rings on the ringer or notification stream, this setting can't help. The decision's diagnostics log which stream was playing. Android gives an app no way to stop another app's vibration, so the buzz lasts until Dasher closes the declined offer. Declines that don't finish within 5 seconds are reported.

Touching the screen while a decline is in progress hands the offer back, and so does any tap of yours on Dasher during it ("View offer details", "Go back", "Cancel", the offer card, any button) and going back to the offer from Dasher's question. Going back counts even when Android reports neither your touch nor your tap: the same offer showing again after its question, with no tap on the question's Decline taken, is yours. The confirmation is not tapped, that offer is not declined again (from the screen or its notification), the sound comes back, and a toast says so. The touch or tap counts from the moment it lands: it is checked right before every automatic tap, so even a read already under way taps nothing more on that offer. The takeover holds through partly drawn frames of that offer. It ends when Dasher goes idle or to a delivery, when a clearly different offer appears, or after two minutes. DoorDash re-posting its notification while you are in another app does not end it: back in Dasher, the same offer with its countdown going on is still yours. A tiny invisible accessibility overlay notices the touch without receiving it. The app's own taps are accessibility actions, not touches, but Android can report one to that overlay as a touch: a touch during one of the app's taps or within 150 ms after it is that echo, and it is ignored. Each tap has a window of its own, which no later tap or read extends. A tap Dasher reports on any button the app did not tap is always yours, even inside such a window; only a tap on the very button the app tapped (or, when Android names no button, one timed inside an app tap's window) is the app's own. Each kind is noted in the diagnostic log with the time since the app's tap. In split screen (your decision), only a touch on Dasher's half hands the offer back: touching Offer Filter's own half (to look at the map or the constellation) lets the decline go on. Offer Filter's page knows its own touches, and Android tells it and the overlay of the same touch in either order, so for up to a tenth of a second nothing is tapped on that offer while the touch is matched; a touch the page never heard of is yours, on Dasher's half. Android does not say where a touch landed in another app's window, so with another app in the other half a touch there still hands the offer back. With Dasher full screen, or when Android gives no bounds for Dasher's half, every touch counts as before, and a tap Dasher reports is always on Dasher's half. The overlay goes up first thing on the main thread; the confirmation waits for it at most 50 ms after the first Decline tap, then is tapped all the same (a touch is still checked right before the tap). A decline whose confirmation was already tapped can't be undone.

**Decline after 10 seconds** (Settings, 0.6.35; off by default; the owner, 22 October 2026: "we could also make auto decline timing configurable like immediate vs 10s"). With it on, an offer failing your rules on Dasher's screen, with Dasher in front, is declined only once it has shown 10 seconds, so you can take it yourself; with 3 seconds or less on its countdown it is declined at once. The status line says how long it waits. The same touch watch is up meanwhile: a touch on the screen or a tap of yours on Dasher leaves the offer to you, and it is declined neither from the screen nor from its notification. A clearly different offer, or the same offer shown afresh after it went away, waits anew; after the screen goes off or the phone locks the wait starts again, unless the offer is already yours, and turning the switch off while such an offer shows leaves it yours. During Peek, and for an offer whose notification comes while Dasher is not in front, the decline is immediate as before, so Dasher is never left over the app you were in; with Dasher in front, Dasher's notification is neither declined nor hidden while its screen waits.

Dasher's question ("Are you sure you want to decline this offer?") is tried three times at most: when Android refuses the tap, or takes it and Dasher still asks, a refused tap is tried again after 300 ms, while a taken tap waits 2–3 seconds for Dasher to close the question before retrying, at most twice. The authority lasts the offer’s remaining countdown plus three seconds (ten seconds when no countdown was read), timed from the actual tap. A temporary unreadable or still-drawing frame does not cancel it. Then the log says "confirmation not tapped", a toast says Offer Filter couldn't confirm the decline, and the offer is left to you: its history line says "Dasher's question not confirmed; left to you" (whether or not a try was taken, since Dasher still asks), its ticket is stamped YOURS, and it counts as review, not filtered. Once the question has shown, that offer's first Decline is never tapped again. The one exception: Dasher closed a question Offer Filter confirmed, nothing of yours was seen, and the offer still shows at least two seconds later (up to twice a slow read, capped at ten seconds). That is logged as a Dasher glitch and gets one more Decline; a second time, the offer is left to you. The diagnostic log has a line for every look at the question while it is awaited (where it looked, found or why not) and every try (taken, refused and why, or skipped and why). A channel's name does not establish its actual configuration; opt-in diagnostics record actual channel sound, vibration, importance, and presence of a full-screen intent when Android exposes these.

Two alerts can ring, each at most once per offer and only while Dasher is not on screen. Whether Dasher is on screen is what the screen reader last saw, at every read and every half second while Dasher is on screen. Dasher sends no event when another app comes in front of it, so while that says Dasher is on screen, a notification checks Android's windows again itself: one that comes just after you leave Dasher still rings (a check that fails counts as Dasher gone). A proven passing offer rings on **Offers that pass**. An offer that cannot be judged rings on **Offers to check** (DoorDash's background notification shows no pay, or auto-decline is paused) when it gets a card at all: none while Dasher's own notification of it is there to tap and alerts by itself. An offer that passes only below your minimums, under Autopilot's lower bar, is announced on **Offers to check** too, never with the pass chime. It stays silent only when Android shows that Dasher's own post of the offer sounded: from Android 10, Android's record that it last alerted audibly for it at that post; before Android 10, a post that Do Not Disturb let through, at default importance or above, on a channel with a sound, and neither an only-alert-once update nor a group child that alerts through its summary. Then Dasher's alert is the one heard; otherwise (Do Not Disturb, a demoted post, an only-alert-once update, or Android not saying) it rings once. Nothing rings on a replay, a reconnect, or for a known failure. An update of an offer the screen has not read rings only if its first card was silent because Dasher was on screen and Dasher has since left, since it may then be a next offer; while Dasher is on screen, an update of an offer the screen already read adds no card. DoorDash reuses one notification for a store's offers: a re-post with the same text is a new offer once the screen saw the last one end (Dasher idle, a delivery, or the dash over), or, with Dasher off screen, when it comes more than 15 seconds after the last post (an update of an old offer that came that way would be announced as a new one). With Dasher off screen, a re-post with changed text after the screen read the last offer and cleared its card is a new offer too, and may ring once; an unchanged re-post moments later, or a replay, never rings. A same-store re-post while Dasher is on screen, after the screen saw the last offer end, is the notification of the offer on screen: it does not stop that offer's decline (its "Are you sure?" is still tapped). Each such re-post is logged. An offer that could not be judged is never declined merely for missing figures. Peek may open Dasher to read it; otherwise the alert asks you to look. Before 0.4.16 these offers got a silent card, and with DoorDash's own offer channel silenced, background offers went unnoticed.

The app retains original passing/unknown notifications rather than deleting them merely because it tried to post a replacement. Alert permission denial, blocked channels, reconnects, duplicate updates, expiration, and stale removals are handled separately.

## Driving layouts

Two layouts let you drive without juggling Dasher, Offer Filter and your map:

- **Dasher full screen, with the tab.** Offers are read and declined on screen, the tab shows the filter's state, and the guide points to the best area while you wait for offers. A tap on the tab opens Offer Filter; the same tab there goes back.
- **Google Maps (or Maps Go, or Waze) full screen, with Peek.** A background offer brings Dasher up for a moment and goes back to your map; after an offer that showed and ended without your accepting it, **Back to map** returns you with one tap.

**Split screen is retired (0.6.33).** The owner, 22 October 2026: "i say let's get rid of split screen mode. we haven't been able to get the auto resizing to work and it's so buggy." Offer Filter no longer splits the screen with Dasher (the header's Split and Swap in Dasher went, with the split-screen hints on the homepage and the recent-apps steps they opened) and no longer moves the divider for a map (0.6.11 to 0.6.32). Every start of Dasher is plain and full screen. A split you make yourself from recent apps still works as Android makes it: Dasher's half is read like a full screen while it is really in view, not under the keyboard, recent apps, a pop-up window or a system window covering half of it or more (the status and navigation bars, or a notification over its edge, don't count); then Dasher's half is left alone, as when it is off screen, and the log says why, once. Beside a map the tab sits over Dasher's half; beside Offer Filter there is no tab (its half has the mascot). Each screen and alert line in the diagnostic log says where Dasher was, such as `win=split/bottom/ours/48` (split screen, Dasher in the bottom half, Offer Filter's half active, Dasher 48% of the screen's height), `win=full/-/dasher/100` or `win=hidden/-/system/0`, and alert lines also whether the screen was on and locked. Reading Dasher runs on the screen reader's own thread, so Offer Filter's page stays responsive while Dasher's map and countdown move. In a window beside another app, the page stays on screen for a whole dash, so it does less: no tilt sensor, and the steady motion (twinkling, drifting, breathing) draws about ten frames a second instead of every frame (a press or a glide you cause still draws every frame). The page's once-a-second refresh asks Android whether Dasher is installed, alerts and installs are allowed and location is permitted at most every 30 seconds, and again at once when the page comes back from elsewhere; where the phone is, for the map's dot, every 5 seconds while the map shows.

## Offer areas

On from the start (the homepage's ground is the map), and nothing is kept until approximate location is allowed; **Offer map** in Settings turns it off. While on, each new standalone offer with a known pay is added to the square of about 2 km the phone was in when it appeared.

- **Location.** Only approximate location is asked for, never precise. The position is the newest fix the phone already has (no GPS is switched on for this), used only if it is under ten minutes old, and rounded to its square before it is kept.
- **Counting.** An offer seen from its notification and then on screen counts once. Add-ons and offers without a pay are left out. An offer with no fresh location is only counted as "without a location". If that happens, Settings has a **Fix** row asking for location all the time, since Android may share location only while Offer Filter is in front.
- **Ranking.** A square is ranked by pooled pay per mile (all pay over all miles, so one short trip cannot dominate) once three offers with miles came in there.
- **Privacy.** A square is where you were when an offer came in, not where its pickup is. Squares are not in reports (the diagnostic report says only how many), and the map is drawn without map tiles. Neighbourhood names come from the phone's own place lookup (Android's geocoder, which on most phones asks Google's servers): only positions rounded to about half a kilometre are asked about, each once, and the names are kept on the phone; nothing about offers goes with them. **Open in Maps** hands one square's center to your maps app only when you tap it. **Clear history** removes them all, with the decisions, the waiting estimates, the captured text and Autopilot's acceptance-rate reading (one confirm lists them; your rules and Autopilot's settings stay).

## Decision history, diagnostics and feedback

The app keeps the latest 200 offer decisions on the phone and two bounded masked diagnostic logs. It also keeps the latest acceptance rate Dasher showed on its decline question (one whole percent, when it was seen and the offer's figures, never any text) for up to 7 days, whether Autopilot is on or off; it leaves the phone only in Share report, diagnostics sent with feedback and Report this offer, and Clear history removes it. If Autopilot grew your minimums before 0.6.21, it keeps a note of that last growth (when, how much, the minimums and bar before and after; numbers only), which Clear history also removes. For its accepted floor it keeps the figures of offers you accepted yourself (at most 100, each for 7 days; numbers only, never sent), which a change of goal or rules and Clear history remove. **Share report** lets you hand a masked report to an app you choose. Everything else you send goes to the project's own accountless feedback service; [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) says exactly what each path sends and how long each copy is kept. Once a UTC day, unless you turn off **Send an anonymous daily count** in Settings (on by default), the app also sends that service one anonymous count, so the developer can see how many people use it: its version, whether this install was counted before and a random code made new each day so a retry counts once, and nothing else. The service keeps counts, and the install page's Download presses, as daily totals for 400 days; to count each once it also keeps a count's code up to 2 days and, for that day only, a one-way value of the network it came from.

- **Send anonymous feedback** (Settings): pick General, Bug, Idea, Usability, Privacy or Update and write up to 4,000 characters (a counter shows how many). No account, name or email; do not include customer, payment or account details. With **Share anonymous diagnostics** on, it also sends the masked report Share report builds, the timing study's section with it (before 0.6.34, each submission chose with **Attach masked diagnostics**); with it off, only your words, the category and the app's version go, and the dialog says which. **Preview what is sent** shows exactly what will go. Nothing leaves until you tap Send. Once the service accepts it, a dialog shows its short reference with **Copy**; the dialog lists the last 10 references. If it is refused, your words stay in the box.
- **Report this offer** (on an offer's ticket): Misread, Wrong decline, Wrong accept or Other, and an optional note. It sends that offer's figures, decision and masked read lines (every word that is not offer vocabulary reduced to its shape), your current rules and Autopilot's state (on or off, goal, bar, mode and last change, and the acceptance rate it counts with: Dasher's latest, carried forward, or its own estimate), the app's and Android's versions and the minute it was decided. The dialog says what it sends; Send is your choice to send it.
- **Share anonymous diagnostics** (Settings, or the switch at the end of the notice from 0.6.18, which shows your current choice from 0.6.20; off by default; no update turns it on). From 0.6.34 it is one switch for this summary, the diagnostics with feedback (above) and the timing study, which were three choices before, so the update to 0.6.34 turns it off for everyone (the owner, 22 October 2026: "we should combine all diagnostics opt in into one"). With it on, after each dash ends (Dasher shows the dash ended, you end it in Dasher with its End dash, or nothing of the dash was seen for 30 minutes), it sends one masked summary of at most 30,000 characters: versions and phone maker, your switches, the bar and whether Autopilot is on, which minimums are set (never their amounts), counts of offers and problems by fixed category, that dash's decisions timed from its start, and masked log lines only around problems. It never carries your acceptance rate: Autopilot's bar and why it changed can show the rate was below your goal, never what it was. No location, place names or device identifier; at most one a dash and three a day. Turning it off discards what has not been sent, the timing study's data with it.
- **After a crash or a stop for not responding**: the next start (once the notice is accepted; nothing of this is read before) keeps a short summary (the kind of stop and where in the app's code, never an error's message or screen text) for 24 hours. With **Share anonymous diagnostics** on, it goes with the next summary; otherwise the homepage shows "Offer Filter stopped unexpectedly last time · Send report", which opens the feedback dialog with Bug chosen and diagnostics attached for that report only. You still tap Send.

Everything is saved on the phone before it is sent (at most 5 submissions, each for at most 7 days), so no signal or a restart loses nothing; Android sends what waits once a network is available, and the app sends it when it next opens. One that has to wait never holds back another. Each part is masked again with the current rules right before it is sent, and nothing is sent unless the current notice is accepted. The service is a Supabase Edge Function at `https://nwglojlrzfbsibtoqjzc.supabase.co/functions/v1/offer-filter-feedback`. The public client has no database access. It stores no account, name, email, IP address or device identifier, and rate-limits with a one-way, daily-changing value derived from the network. Submissions are deleted after 90 days. This is described as accountless rather than as a guarantee of anonymity, because Cloudflare, Supabase and network providers necessarily handle connection metadata. The developer may review submissions with Anthropic's Claude or OpenAI's ChatGPT/Codex.

The function's source, the production schema and a hardening migration are under `backend/anonymous-feedback/`, with deploy steps in its README. Nothing there is deployed automatically.

Versions before 0.5.0 could connect to GitHub, and the reports they filed went to the project's public GitHub repository. On its first run, 0.5.0 deletes that connection, its history, the old report queue and its jobs, once; the app sends nothing to GitHub any more.

## Build and release

Use the [resumable local release pipeline](docs/release-pipeline.md) for new releases: `python3 tools/release.py`. It preserves complete source and original test evidence, checkpoints each completed phase, and resumes without rebuilding or signing again. Build, test, package and sign locally in the existing workspace; hosted CI or provider builds require a separate explicit exception. The legacy primitives below remain its verification authorities.

Render serves the build committed to `release/`: `render-build.sh` runs `tools/mirror_repo_feed.py`, which publishes `release/` (signed with the cloud key by `tools/sign-local.sh` and checked by `tools/publish-repo-feed.py`) as `public/` after checking its size and SHA-256 against `release/latest.json`, that its signing block names the cloud certificate, and that it is not older than what Render serves. It builds and signs nothing, so it takes seconds of build time. The old source build is `tools/render-build-full.sh`: it installs the SDK/JDK as needed, verifies the current public channel using the production Java downloader, runs the tests, signs with the cloud key and writes `public/` through `tools/finalize_release.py`.

Update checks are always automatic: Settings has no switch for them (an "off" an older version kept is turned back on, once, and logged). They are spaced: opening the app, the retry job and a tap on **Updates** in Settings check at once (after the one-minute cooldown that follows a check, or the backoff after a failure); coming back to the app, a day-and-night recreation (a resize only lays the page out again), a service reconnecting and the periodic job wait five minutes after the last attempt. While you are dashing, an update an automatic check found waits ("Update ready: installs after your dash", looked at again every five minutes): installing closes Offer Filter, and with it its half of a split screen beside Dasher. A pause, a quiet half hour, lock or service interruption never counts as permission to install: an observed dash stays held until Dasher positively shows it ended (or you end it there with End dash), or until nothing of a dash has been seen for 8 hours with the screen off and Dasher not in front, when the update installs after a fresh check and every verification step (the log says "dash hold reached its ceiling"). A previously verified update avoids repeated downloads while held; before installation it still follows the normal feed and APK verification path. While no dash is on, a held update shows **Update ready · Install now** on the homepage. A tap on **Updates** can override the dash hold, except while Dasher is on screen during a dash. Dasher on its home screen before a dash (its **Dash** button) holds no update back, in the other half of a split screen too. While Android doesn't allow installs from Offer Filter, the homepage's **Allow updates** step and one quiet notification per version lead to Android's "Allow from this source". If Android blocks an install (Samsung's Auto Blocker, say), Settings → Updates says so. The updating screen offers "Still updating? Continue" after a minute. After an update installed while Offer Filter was open, it reopens only over its own screen or the home screen, never over Dasher or your map.

`app/build.gradle` is the version source of truth. A changed source commit cannot reuse the live version code. A deliberate same-source redeploy reuses the already published APK/feed bytes after checking them, preventing mutable content under one version. `verification.json` reports test totals and the exact live version reached by the production-transport probe. The app trusts one release origin, the Render host, with one exact feed and one exact APK address, and checks every redirect hop against them. The repository's `release/` folder is Render's input, not a feed the app reads; `release/latest.json` keeps its old repository APK address only for phones on 0.4.72 and older. The retired GitHub feeds are neither trusted by the app nor published to by any script in this repository. The archived manual-tests workflow never signs or publishes an APK: a gated source change still needs the original-key signing and the `release/` commit that Render mirrors.

When Render cannot build (for example when the workspace is out of build minutes), `tools/sign-local.sh` builds and signs the same release in a Claude session. The same cloud key reaches the session only through the environment variables `OFFER_FILTER_KEYSTORE_B64` and `OFFER_FILTER_SIGNING_PASSWORD`, which the user sets in the environment settings. The script refuses any other certificate, runs the tests, and leaves `dist/OfferFilter-<version>.apk`, for publication through the existing automatic update feeds, with settings kept.

To ship that APK, commit the source and run `tools/publish-repo-feed.py`. It refuses an uncommitted source, any other signer, a package or embedded version that disagrees with `app/build.gradle`, a version code not above the one already in `release/`, and any conflict with a freshly verified Render version; it checks the feed as Render will serve it with the app's own policy code. The retired full Render publisher also checks the repository feed so the two paths cannot reuse a version with different source or bytes. It then writes `release/OfferFilter.apk` and `release/latest.json`, naming the source commit. Commit `release/` on its own and push both commits to `main`; Render's next build mirrors it to every phone. Render's auto-deploy is off, so trigger that build by hand, check it with `tools/verify_channel.py`, and then bring the website's version up to date (`tools/sync-release.py --live` in offer-filter-site). Nothing is signed on GitHub, and no workflow or paid service is involved.

### Accountless updates

Phones use the public Render mirror only. Update package/version/size/SHA-256/signing-certificate verification is unchanged, and the original signing identity is still required. The repository `release/` pair remains the source artifact that the Render mirror serves, but the Android app no longer authenticates users to GitHub or fetches a second feed from the repository.

### Focused local development checks

Only the safety, privacy and consent suites remain: the owner had the other unit tests removed on 22 October 2026 (the branch claude/last-with-unit-tests keeps the last commit that had them). Do not write new tests. When a deliberate change makes a kept suite wrong, correct that suite and say so in the release notes. Use explicit test classes or methods while editing, then run the complete daily suite before handoff:

```sh
tools/test-dev.sh PrivacyBoundaryTest
tools/test-dev.sh PrivacyBoundaryTest.paymentScreenNeverReachesEitherLogOrDecisionEvidence
tools/test-dev.sh ActionSafetyTest ConsentGateTest
tools/test-dev.sh --all
```

The helper uses the existing Android 15 (API 35) profile and quick JIT, retaining Gradle's warm daemon and caches.
It accepts short or fully qualified class names, with an optional method name, and rejects arbitrary Gradle options.
Missing classes or unmatched methods fail. `--all` runs the complete daily suite; focused checks do not replace it
or the full release gate.

`-PdevChecks` puts JUnit XML and binary results under `app/build/test-results/devChecks/testDebugUnitTest/`, and HTML
under `app/build/reports/tests/devChecks/testDebugUnitTest/`. The helper leaves the full run's JUnit, binary, HTML,
and lint reports in place. It also uses `.gradle/dev-checks` for separate project execution and configuration history,
while sharing the existing Gradle user-home dependency/build caches and daemon. This adds first-use setup for the
development history. The development flag and cache directory must be used together; combining `-PdevChecks` with
`-PallSdks` is rejected. Run one Gradle command at a time per checkout: compilation and other intermediates are shared.
The original Gradle commands retain their existing output locations; use the helper when development checks must
preserve the previous full reports.

Both test profiles also track the repository legal documents that LegalTextsTest reads, so changes to those bytes
invalidate cached test results. Preserved full reports describe the inputs they
tested: every release still runs `testDebugUnitTest -PallSdks` and `lintDebug -PallSdks` through the existing gate
against frozen inputs. This helper does not sign or publish.

### Low-memory local validation

Run Android tests and builds only in the authorized local workspace. For a memory-constrained machine, run one checkout at a time with one Gradle worker and one test JVM:

```sh
./gradlew --no-daemon --no-parallel --max-workers=1 \
  '-Dorg.gradle.jvmargs=-Xmx512m -XX:+UseParallelGC -Dfile.encoding=UTF-8' \
  -Pforks=1 -PtestHeap=768m -PallSdks testDebugUnitTest lintDebug
```

`org.gradle.jvmargs` caps the Gradle build JVM's heap; `testHeap` separately caps each test JVM's heap. `testHeap` defaults to the existing `1536m` and accepts a positive whole number followed by `m` or `g` (case-insensitive). Empty or malformed values fail configuration instead of silently falling back. `forks` still defaults to 3 when omitted. These opt-in limits do not change the shared defaults in `gradle.properties`.

The command keeps the existing full test selection: `-PallSdks` removes the day-to-day API 35 restriction so tests use their declared SDKs, API 35 and 36 (Android 15 and 16). Neither the SDK declarations nor test assertions are changed. Lint remains part of this local check; signing, publishing and real-phone verification still require their separate existing gates.

To check that the tests actually execute within the smaller heap, add `--rerun-tasks --no-build-cache` to the command. An `UP-TO-DATE` or `FROM-CACHE` result alone does not demonstrate that the lower-memory test run succeeds.

These are Java heap limits, not a total RAM cap: native memory, metaspace, Android tools and other processes need additional space. A smaller heap can run slower or still run out of memory. If that happens, preserve the failure log and retry locally with a larger explicit heap or more available RAM; do not skip tests or move Android execution to a cloud build service.

## Current limitations

No official DoorDash API integration, hidden-background UI access, global audio muting, or device-level vibration suppression is implemented. Notification identifiers are not guaranteed unique order IDs. Acceptance is seen from the user's Accept tap (which Dasher may not report) or, without it, from an offer closing with time left into a delivery screen Dasher's words show, after the app read Dasher's wait for offers before that offer; a delivery screen worded in a way the app does not know, an offer that came during a delivery or with no wait for offers read before it (after a restart, say), or one accepted while Dasher was not on screen counts nothing and may leave route context unknown. Whether Dasher reports finger taps, and its wording after an Accept, are unverified on a real phone until a report shows them. Autopilot reads the acceptance rate only when Dasher's decline question shows exactly one percent beside acceptance-rate wording; that, and whether it is the rate before or after the decline counts, is unverified on a real phone. Android may defer periodic/one-shot work or require an update-installation confirmation. Changing these limits needs additional evidence or capabilities, not more optimistic labels in the UI.
