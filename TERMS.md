If you do not accept them, do not use the app: tap Not now, turn off its screen reading and notification access, or uninstall it.

## Acceptance-rate risk

Automatic declines may dramatically lower your DoorDash acceptance rate. This can affect DoorDash programs that depend on it. You must acknowledge this risk before filtering begins, including after an update that changes the notice. Continuing is your choice, at your own risk; the app makes no promise to preserve your acceptance rate. Autopilot's default goal, pay first, does not try to keep your acceptance rate up. An acceptance-rate goal you choose instead is a best effort, not a promise: Autopilot cannot accept offers for you, and your acceptance rate rises only when you accept offers. Reach Platinum (from 0.6.60) aims for the acceptance rate the app works out from the numbers on Dasher's Ratings page, plus 2 points; that working-out is an estimate of how DoorDash scores ratings, which DoorDash can change at any time, and it never promises a tier. Before 0.6.62, an offer you accepted or declined yourself could also hold Autopilot's bar above what the goal allowed for the next 24 offers; from 0.6.62 the bar is never above your minimums, and from 0.6.64 it drops only for an acceptance-rate goal or when you accept an offer below 100% (your minimums, or from 0.6.68 your best accept).

## What it does

- Time-aware scoring (from 0.6.46) is optional and starts off, in How offers are judged. It compares the value of retained standalone offers near the current local time with offers overall, using your current minimums for every comparison. It can raise or lower a standalone score by at most 25%, and therefore change which offers are declined in either Strict or Area mode, with or without Autopilot. It uses up to the last 200 retained offers from the last 28 days, including declined offers, with more weight on recent days; add-ons, paused observations, skipped stores, apparent misreads and unread scores are excluded. It needs at least 30 eligible offers overall and 12 within 90 minutes of the local time across at least three dates. Until then it uses the normal score. Estimates use medians and are pulled toward no adjustment when evidence is sparse. Updates apply only between offers at the existing safe point. Your minimums are unchanged; the existing hard maximum stops, unknown-fact review, strict add-on checks and separate strict automatic-acceptance checks remain. Offer details show the applied factor, sample counts and score before adjustment. Turning the switch off restores scoring without this adjustment. Local history is evidence of past offers, not a forecast or promise about earnings.
- It reads the Dasher app's screen and notifications on your phone, through Android's accessibility service and notification access, which you turn on yourself. From 0.6.57 it reads each offer even with no rule set (tapping nothing), so that an offer you accept can set your minimums: until 0.6.68 only while acceptance learning was on (with it off and no rule set, it read nothing of Dasher's screen); from 0.6.68 learning is always on. While auto-decline is paused (from 0.6.35), it still reads each offer on Dasher's screen and shows its score and what auto-decline would do, and taps nothing; offers read while paused do not count for Autopilot, but from 0.6.59 an offer you accept yourself while paused sets your minimums as when auto-decline is on. Turning Offer Filter off in Android's Accessibility settings stops all reading of Dasher's screen.
- It compares each offer with your active minimums: pay per stop, pay per mile and pay per hour of Dasher's time estimate, plus an optional maximum number of stops. Pay per stop (from 0.6.12, when it replaced the minimum pay, keeping its number) is asked for each stop Dasher shows, the pickup included (from 0.6.14): twice for a single order, which Dasher counts as two stops, three times for three stops, four times for four, so $5 a stop asks $10 of a single order and $15 of three stops. When 0.6.14 began counting the pickup, your number was not changed, so a single order asks twice what it did under 0.6.12 and 0.6.13. When an offer's stops are not read, it stays for review unless its pay misses even one stop's worth or another minimum. Area mode is the default (from 0.6.27, and set once then for earlier installs, whose Strict could not be told from the earlier default): stronger pay or rates can compensate for weaker ones in the combined score. Strict mode checks every active minimum; choose it in How offers are judged, the word in the radar's middle on the homepage. Maximum stops remains a hard limit, missing required facts stay for review, and add-ons remain strict. An offer permitted by the filter is not an accepted delivery. When an offer fails the chosen rules, the app can tap Decline and Dasher's confirmation for you. With Decline after 10 seconds on in Settings (from 0.6.35; off unless you turn it on), an offer shown on Dasher's screen while Dasher is in front is declined only once it has shown for 10 seconds, or at once when its countdown has 3 seconds or less left; your touch on the screen or a tap on Dasher in that time leaves it to you. Offers it handles while you are in another app, from Dasher's notification or with Peek, are still declined at once. From 0.6.18 it confirms only a decline question worded as Dasher's usual one ("Are you sure you want to decline this offer?"), seen in the developer's own reports; any other question, or one saying declines are limited or that another decline would end your dash, is left to you and auto-decline pauses until you resume it. An offer already left to you stays yours if you change your rules while it shows; the new rules judge the next offer. Autopilot is on by default (from 0.6.2, and turned on once then for earlier installs), with pay first as its goal (from 0.6.18, when it replaced the suggested acceptance-rate goal of 70%: once, a 70% goal moved to pay first, since it could not be told from the suggested one, and a 50% goal stayed) until you choose a goal that protects your acceptance rate (Reach Platinum, 70% or 50%); you can turn it off from its chip on the homepage. From 0.6.62 (the one-minimums model) Autopilot applies one bar, between 10% and 100% on the scale scores use (your minimums, or from 0.6.68 your best accept, below), using your recent offers, how often they come and the acceptance rate Dasher shows: with pay first it stays at 100% unless fewer than one in five of your last 100 offers would pass, and with an acceptance-rate goal it is the highest bar, at most 100%, at which enough of them would pass for the goal. From 0.6.64 it is also never above the bar your own accepts set (see Acceptance learning). It never asks more than your minimums, and it never changes them. An offer's score is its pay as a share of what your minimums ask of it (from 0.6.64; in 0.6.62 and 0.6.63, of your minimums at the bar). From 0.6.68, 100% is your best accept: the highest such share of an offer you accepted yourself among the offers the app keeps, held to at most 100% of your minimums. Every score is then that share as a percent of your best accept's, so the best offer you accepted scores 100%, and the bar is a percent on the same scale; with no accepted offer to go by, 100% is your minimums themselves. Your best accept is worked out again from your history whenever it or your minimums change, and Clear history removes it. Because it follows your minimums, raising one minimum also lowers your best accept's share of them, so it changes how pay per stop, per mile and per hour are weighed more than how much an offer must pay overall. An offer passes at the bar or above, and an offer below 100% that passes a lowered bar says so; it is left to you unless you turned on auto-accept. The chart scores every offer on that scale against your current minimums and draws the bar as a line, so a tree above it would pass now, with a line where your minimums themselves stand (above 100% while your best accept scores under them). From 0.6.29 to 0.6.61 Autopilot also kept separate working minimums set by your accepts and held its bar, between 10% and 150%, at an accepted offer's score or just above an offer you declined, for the next 24 offers; 0.6.62 removes those working minimums and holds, and an offer you accept yourself now sets your minimums instead (see Acceptance learning). From 0.5.1 to 0.6.20 Autopilot also raised saved minimums, at most 10% at a time, with Let my minimums grow to turn that off; that growth and its switch were removed in 0.6.21. A growth made before 0.6.21 keeps its note on the homepage, with Undo, until you tap it or your minimums change another way. Auto-accept starts off. If you separately enable it in Settings, it can tap Accept on any complete standalone offer the filter passes, by the same rule, score and bar that judge every offer, committing you to that delivery (from 0.6.68). So when Autopilot lowers its bar for an acceptance-rate goal, it can accept offers well below your best accept. Before 0.6.68 it asked every active minimum strictly at 100% or more, even in area mode, and never took an offer that passed only a lowered bar. Automatic acceptance leaves add-ons and unclear offers to you. Add-ons are judged strictly: the whole route after the add-on against your minimums at the current bar, on the same scale as every offer (from 0.6.68, your best accept's).
- It also declines a standalone offer from a store you put on your skip list (from an offer's details; Settings → Skipped stores removes one), whatever it pays, when it reads that store's name on Dasher's screen. An offer from two stores is declined when either is on the list; an offer whose store it cannot read is judged by your rules as usual. Store names can be misread.
- Acceptance learning is always on (from 0.6.68; before, on by default, with a Learn button on the homepage that turned it off and on, now gone). Each positively confirmed manual standalone acceptance (an offer you accepted yourself, including one you took back from the app's decline before it tapped Dasher's confirmation) changes your minimums or the bar. From 0.6.68, each active minimum it beat (pay per stop, the pickup included, pay per mile or pay per hour) rises to that offer's own rate, rounded down to the cent, whatever it scores, so your minimums are the highest each measure has been on an offer you accepted; the others stay, and it never lowers one. (From 0.6.64 until 0.6.68, only an accept scoring 100% or more of your minimums raised them; in 0.6.62 an accept set each minimum to its own rate, up or down, and in 0.6.63 it moved all of them together by its score.) Then the bar your accepts set follows its score with those minimums: if it scores 100% or more (it is your best accept), that bar goes back to 100%, unless you are below your acceptance-rate goal; if it scores under 100%, that bar becomes its score, so offers like it pass; after an earlier accept that scored less, that raises it, though Autopilot's bar still goes no higher than your goal allows. With no minimum set, an acceptance sets each to what that offer paid on it. A measure you turned off stays off. A note on the homepage says what changed, with Undo, which puts back your minimums from before that acceptance; Undo last change in How offers are judged does the same (from 0.6.68; before, in Learned minimums). Undo is conditional and cannot overwrite a later change of yours, Clear history or another acceptance. Merely passing, timing out or disappearing is not an acceptance. Automatic accepts, declines, add-ons, ambiguous outcomes and offers whose pay was not read do not change them; a paused auto-decline does not stop it (from 0.6.59). Before 0.6.62, learning kept separate best-ever learned minimums that only rose and applied alongside your saved minimums (from 0.6.61 a raise became your saved minimum at once; before, a note offered Adopt); 0.6.62 makes any learned minimum still asking more than a saved one your saved minimum, once, and then removes the learned bests. From 0.6.54 Clear history sets your minimums back to the typical ones ($2.00 a stop, $1.00 a mile and $15 an hour); your other rules stay. Learning is local and makes no promise about earnings or future offers.
- If Dasher reports an error after that first Decline and leaves a recognized empty or map-only screen, it can press Back and retry the same offer, at most twice within the original attempt's time limit. It checks the offer again before retrying. Your touch, a new offer, locking the phone or leaving Dasher stops this recovery; an unknown loading screen alone never permits it.
- Peek is on by default after you accept the current notice. On an unlocked, quiet phone it can briefly open Dasher for a fresh background offer and apply your normal rules. It can return to the app you were using after completion evidence; while navigation is detected, it also returns for passing or unclear offers and leaves a card. Otherwise those offers stay in Dasher. If Dasher opens without showing the offer's details, Peek may tap Dasher's ⚙ button once when Dasher shows Finding offers, else Dasher's own offer notification once; if they still don't show, it leaves a card saying so and, while you navigate, returns to your map first. When Dasher is already on screen (in front or beside the app) still showing its wait for offers 5 seconds after an offer's notification, with none of that offer drawn, Peek may tap Dasher's ⚙ once the same way (on Finding offers, after 2 seconds without your touch), else that notification once; never over another of Dasher's screens or after you touch Dasher. Your touch on Dasher or an app switch ends that automatic return (a touch made before Dasher appeared, meant for the app you were in, still lets a completed automatic decline take you back). If the phone locks during Peek, Peek waits up to 60 seconds for you to unlock it; an offer that arrives while the phone is locked can be opened when you unlock within 40 seconds of it, unless you open Dasher yourself first. Within a minute of an offer's first notification, a re-post of it does not open Dasher again when you already had that offer in Dasher or may have accepted it. After repeated withdrawn offers or failed launches, Peek pauses itself until your next dash or for 15 minutes; after an error, until your next dash or until you tap Resume on the homepage. Peek never turns off its Settings switch. You can turn Peek off in Settings.
- When you tap one of its offer cards, it opens Dasher; if Dasher comes up without the offer, it may tap Dasher's ⚙ button once on its Finding offers screen (after 2 seconds without your touch), else Dasher's own offer notification once. After an offer that left you in Dasher from a navigation app ends without being accepted, a Back to map button can take you back; it opens your map only when you tap it.
- During an active dash with auto-decline on and a rule set, it keeps your unlocked screen from timing out, whatever app is in front, while it has seen something of the dash in the last 15 minutes or Dasher shows it, unless the dash is paused in Dasher, a call is under way, battery saver is on, or the battery is at 20% or less and not charging. It never wakes or unlocks the phone; the power button still turns the screen off. This uses more battery.
- While it declines an offer on screen, it can turn media and alarm sound down for a moment and then put them back. During Peek, a delivery or turn-by-turn navigation it touches only the alarm stream, leaving media and navigation audio alone. You can turn this off in Settings.
- A tap is a request to the Dasher app, not a confirmation from DoorDash. Hiding a notification does not decline an order.
- It updates itself from its public signed update feed without requiring an account. An update it finds by itself during a dash waits until the dash ends; if nothing of a dash is seen for 8 hours while Dasher is not in front and the screen is off, it installs then, after checking the update again.
- Use Feedback in Settings to send general feedback or choose a recent offer; Send feedback on an offer's ticket opens the same screen with that offer selected. Feedback and offer reports are accountless and are sent only when you tap Send. Masked diagnostics go only with Share anonymous diagnostics (off by default) turned on: after each dash and with the feedback you send. From 0.6.34 that one switch covers every diagnostic, the offer timing study included, and the update turns it off for everyone until they turn it on. A report the app offers after a stop sends them once, when you tap Send. The privacy text says what each sends, where it goes and how long it is kept: unsent submissions wait on the phone for at most 7 days, and the feedback service keeps them for 90 days. Sending feedback does not guarantee a reply, review or fix.
- Once a day it sends the feedback service one anonymous count, so the developer can see how many people use the app: its version, whether this install was counted before and a random code made new each day. It is on by default; turn it off with Send an anonymous daily count in Settings. The privacy text says what it sends and how the service keeps it (as daily totals for 400 days, and briefly what lets each count once).
