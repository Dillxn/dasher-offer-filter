## Acceptance-rate risk

Automatic declines may dramatically lower your DoorDash acceptance rate. This can affect DoorDash programs that depend on it. You must acknowledge this risk before filtering begins, including after an update that changes the notice. Continuing is your choice, at your own risk; the app makes no promise to preserve your acceptance rate. Autopilot's acceptance-rate goal is a best effort, not a promise: Autopilot cannot accept offers for you, and your acceptance rate rises only when you accept offers. An offer you accept yourself can also hold Autopilot's bar above what the goal allows.

## What it does

- It reads the Dasher app's screen and notifications on your phone, through Android's accessibility service and notification access, which you turn on yourself. While auto-decline is paused, or no rule is set, it reads nothing of Dasher's screen.
- It compares each offer with your active minimums: pay per stop, pay per mile and pay per hour of Dasher's time estimate, plus an optional maximum number of stops. Pay per stop (from 0.6.12, when it replaced the minimum pay, keeping its number) is asked for each stop after the first pickup: once for a single order, which Dasher counts as two stops, twice for three stops, three times for four, so a stacked offer must pay more. When an offer's stops are not read, it stays for review unless its pay misses even one stop's worth or another minimum. Strict mode is the default and checks every active minimum. Area mode is a separate, initially off choice: stronger pay or rates can compensate for weaker ones in the combined score. Maximum stops remains a hard limit, missing required facts stay for review, and add-ons remain strict. An offer permitted by the filter is not an accepted delivery. When an offer fails the chosen rules, the app can tap Decline and Dasher's confirmation for you. An offer already left to you stays yours if you change your rules while it shows; the new rules judge the next offer. Autopilot is on by default (from 0.6.2, and turned on once then for earlier installs, keeping a goal chosen before), with an acceptance-rate goal of 70% until you choose another; you can turn it off from its chip on the homepage. While it is on, it applies one bar to the chosen scoring model and resolved saved/learned requirements, between 50% and 150%, using your recent offers, how often they come and the acceptance rate Dasher shows when you decline. When you accept a standalone offer yourself (the confirmed acceptances acceptance learning counts, whether or not learning is on) and its score under your current rules is above the bar, Autopilot raises the bar to that score (at most 150%) at its next safe point and keeps it at least there for the rest of that dash, even above what your acceptance goal allows, which can mean more declines and a lower acceptance rate. A new dash, turning Autopilot on, or a change of your goal, minimums, max stops, mode or learning ends that hold; Adopt keeps it. Above 100% it declines offers that meet your minimums. Offers it lets through below your minimums are left to you. Autopilot also raises your minimums once its bar has stayed at 103% or more for 30 offers over at least 2 days (bars an accepted offer held up do not count): all of them by the same share, at most 10% at a time, while its bar comes down so offers are asked about the same right after. It never lowers them; you can. A note on the homepage says what grew, with Undo, until you tap it or your minimums change another way. This bar-based growth waits while an enabled learned floor exceeds its saved minimum, so growing saved knobs and lowering the bar cannot loosen a learned requirement. Raised minimums can mean more declines. Turn this off with Let my minimums grow in Autopilot's details; while Autopilot is off, it does not grow your saved minimums. Auto-accept starts off. If you separately enable it in Settings, it can tap Accept on a complete standalone offer that strictly meets every active saved and enabled learned minimum at 100% or any higher Autopilot bar, even in area mode, committing you to that delivery. Automatic acceptance leaves add-ons and unclear offers to you. Add-on filtering stays strict against saved minimums at the current bar; standalone learned floors do not apply to add-ons unless adopted as saved minimums.
- It also declines a standalone offer from a store you put on your skip list (from an offer's details; Settings → Skipped stores removes one), whatever it pays, when it reads that store's name on Dasher's screen. An offer from two stores is declined when either is on the list; an offer whose store it cannot read is judged by your rules as usual. Store names can be misread. Autopilot can weigh your own cost per mile, off unless you set it in its details.
- Acceptance learning is separate from area mode and Autopilot, and is on by default; the Learn button on the homepage turns it off and on. While filtering and learning are on, each positively confirmed manual standalone acceptance (an offer you accepted yourself, including one you took back from the app's decline before it tapped Dasher's confirmation, when it came while you were waiting for offers) can keep your best accepted pay per stop (an offer with that offer's stops must then pay one cent more than it did, and other numbers of stops in proportion; an acceptance whose stops were not read teaches nothing on pay) and independent exact best pay-per-mile and pay-per-minute rates. When a new best asks more than a saved minimum, offers must meet that learned minimum as well, which can mean more declines; your saved minimums stay as you set them. A note on the homepage says what rose, with Adopt, which makes those learned minimums your saved ones; OK closes the note. Undo of an adoption is in Learned minimums; it is conditional and cannot overwrite later changes. Pay per mile learns only from trips of at least 2 miles and pay per minute from at least 10 minutes. Automatic accepts, declines, add-ons and ambiguous outcomes do not teach it. Unknown facts do not teach their axes; any known implausible figure rejects the lesson. Learned bests can activate an unset money minimum. Off or paused retains learned bests without new learning. Confirmed Reset clears active bests and Undo; prior decision snapshots remain until Clear history. Clear history clears both active learning and that history. Adopt raises only lower saved minimums, which then apply to add-ons too. Reset and Clear history keep your minimums as they are. Learning is local and makes no promise about earnings or future offers.
- If Dasher reports an error after that first Decline and leaves a recognized empty or map-only screen, it can press Back and retry the same offer, at most twice within the original attempt's time limit. It checks the offer again before retrying. Your touch, a new offer, locking the phone or leaving Dasher stops this recovery; an unknown loading screen alone never permits it.
- In split screen during a delivery, it moves the divider, using the divider's own controls (the ones Android gives screen readers), to give the navigating app more room, and puts it back afterward; if you move the divider yourself, it stops for that dash. It never moves it while an offer is up or during Dasher's own turn-by-turn navigation. Some phones' dividers offer no such controls; then nothing moves.
- Peek is on by default after you accept the current notice. On an unlocked, quiet phone it can briefly open Dasher for a fresh background offer and apply your normal rules. It can return to the app you were using after completion evidence; while navigation is detected, it also returns for passing or unclear offers and leaves a card. Otherwise those offers stay in Dasher. If Dasher opens without showing the offer's details, Peek may tap Dasher's own offer notification once; if they still don't show, it leaves a card saying so and, while you navigate, returns to your map first. When Dasher is already on screen (in front or beside the app) still showing its wait for offers 5 seconds after an offer's notification, with none of that offer drawn, Peek may tap that notification once the same way; never over another of Dasher's screens or after you touch Dasher. Your touch on Dasher or an app switch ends that automatic return (a touch made before Dasher appeared, meant for the app you were in, still lets a completed automatic decline take you back). If the phone locks during Peek, Peek waits up to 60 seconds for you to unlock it; an offer that arrives while the phone is locked can be opened when you unlock within 40 seconds of it, unless you open Dasher yourself first. Within a minute of an offer's first notification, a re-post of it does not open Dasher again when you already had that offer in Dasher or may have accepted it. After repeated withdrawn offers or failed launches, Peek pauses itself until your next dash or for 15 minutes; after an error, until your next dash or until you tap Resume on the homepage. Peek never turns off its Settings switch. You can turn Peek off in Settings.
- When you tap one of its offer cards, it opens Dasher; if Dasher comes up without the offer, it may tap Dasher's own offer notification once. After an offer that left you in Dasher from a navigation app ends without being accepted, a Back to map button can take you back; it opens your map only when you tap it.
- During an active dash with auto-decline on and a rule set, it keeps your unlocked screen from timing out, whatever app is in front, while it has seen something of the dash in the last 15 minutes or Dasher shows it, unless the dash is paused in Dasher, a call is under way, battery saver is on, or the battery is at 20% or less and not charging. It never wakes or unlocks the phone; the power button still turns the screen off. This uses more battery.
- While it declines an offer on screen, it can turn media and alarm sound down for a moment and then put them back. During Peek, a delivery or turn-by-turn navigation it touches only the alarm stream, leaving media and navigation audio alone. You can turn this off in Settings.
- A tap is a request to the Dasher app, not a confirmation from DoorDash. Hiding a notification does not decline an order.
- It updates itself from its public signed update feed without requiring an account. An update it finds by itself during a dash waits until the dash ends; if nothing of a dash is seen for 8 hours while Dasher is not in front and the screen is off, it installs then, after checking the update again.
- Feedback and offer reports are accountless and are sent only when you tap Send. Masked diagnostics go only when you attach them to feedback, or after each dash once you turn on Share anonymous diagnostics after each dash (off by default). The privacy text says what each sends, where it goes and how long it is kept: unsent submissions wait on the phone for at most 7 days, and the feedback service keeps them for 90 days. Sending feedback does not guarantee a reply, review or fix.
- Once a day it sends the feedback service one anonymous count, so the developer can see how many people use the app: its version, whether this install was counted before and a random code made new each day. It is on by default; turn it off with Send an anonymous daily count in Settings. The privacy text says what it sends and how the service keeps it (as daily totals for 400 days, and briefly what lets each count once).
